# Journalists' Day-to-Day Workflow Pain Points: Korean Surveys First, Global Surveys for Comparison

Scope note: these notes were compiled 2026-11-15. Korea is the primary focus, with global data as comparison. Every figure has an inline source. Older data is labelled with its year. Figures that could not be confirmed against a primary source are flagged.

---

## 1. What do the latest Korean journalist surveys say about workload, hours, article counts, stress, AI use and burdensome tasks?

### Takeaway
The latest KPF survey, '2025 한국의 언론인' (n=2,020, fieldwork Jun–Sep 2025), finds that Korean reporters file about 23.6 articles a week, and 38.5 at wire services. Morale is collapsing: 76.2% say morale has fallen, and the KJA's July 2026 poll puts it at 87.3%. 45.5% feel fatigue from digital-transition demands, and 70.8% of those blame the increased workload. Low pay is the top morale complaint. "Excessive workload and labour intensity" is cited by about 46.5%. The 2025 survey's daily-working-hours figure could not be found in any accessible coverage.

### Cited Findings
**KPF '2025 한국의 언론인' (17th journalist survey; Hankook Research; n=2,020; June–Sept 2025)**
- Average weekly articles per reporter is 23.6. By outlet type: wire/news agency 38.5, internet outlets 27.4, newspapers 21.5, broadcasters 19.2. Rank-and-file reporters average 21.6, reporters in their 50s 26.6, and those 60+ 37.6. — [미디어오늘](https://www.mediatoday.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=331660); also [Daum/미디어오늘](https://v.daum.net/v/20260120201503201?f=p)
- 45.5% feel fatigue from digital response and innovation demands. Of those, 70.8% cite increased workload as the reason. — [미디어오늘](https://www.mediatoday.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=331660)
- Average sleep is 377.3 minutes (about 6 h 17 min) per day. — [미디어오늘](https://www.mediatoday.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=331660)
- 76.2% say reporters' morale fell in the last 1–2 years. The top causes (multiple response) are low pay and benefits 65.8%, lack of vision as a journalist 56.8%, and lack of sense of achievement 33.5%. — [미디어오늘](https://www.mediatoday.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=331660)
- Satisfaction on an 11-point scale is 6.13 for the current job, 6.06 for journalism as a profession and 5.53 for the employer. 61.0% would choose journalism again. — [미디어오늘](https://www.mediatoday.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=331660)
- 58.1% use generative AI in their work, rising to 63.1% at internet outlets, 71.6% among reporters in their 20s and 72.1% among those with under 5 years' tenure. — [미디어오늘](https://www.mediatoday.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=331660); [기자협회보 search summary / 61238](https://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61238)
- About 4 in 10 say AI could threaten job stability. AI is used mainly for analysis, classification, translation and proofreading. — [기자협회보](https://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61238)
- 35.6% have been harassed because of an article. 60.2% say the growing influence of current-affairs YouTube is negative for journalism. — [미디어오늘](https://www.mediatoday.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=331660)
- Average salary is KRW 59.74M. Internet outlets average KRW 49.14M and regional dailies KRW 40.28M. — [미디어오늘](https://www.mediatoday.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=331660)
- 36.6% work from home at least sometimes, rising to 58.9% at internet outlets. — [미디어오늘](https://www.mediatoday.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=331660)

**Working-hours trend (older KPF waves)**
- Average daily working hours: 10.6 h in 2013, 10.1 h in 2017, 9.2 h in 2019 and 8.9 h in 2021 (KPF '한국의 언론인 2021'). No 2025 figure was found in coverage. — [KPF 한국의 언론인 2021 notice](https://www.kpf.or.kr/front/board/boardContentsView.do?board_id=246&contents_id=8330465d276a418a803e56d563763de5)
- An older KJA article ("주 52시간 근무하는 세상…언론사는 주 70시간", year not verified, likely 2018) documented 70-hour weeks at some outlets. — [기자협회보](http://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=43752)

**Korea Journalists Association (한국기자협회) 62nd-anniversary poll (Macromill Embrain; n=1,625 of 11,439 members reached; fieldwork 21–29 July 2026; ±2.43%p)**
- 87.3% say morale has fallen (32.5% "greatly"), up from 77.7% the previous year. Only 1.6% say it has risen. — [기자협회보 61442](http://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61442)
- Reasons for low morale (multiple response): low pay and benefits 67.4%, falling social reputation 54.3%, shrinking influence of the press 52.2%, excessive workload and labour intensity 46.5%, and lack of achievement and satisfaction 45.0%. — [기자협회보 61442](http://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61442)
- Job satisfaction is 45.9% (down 1.5%p) and dissatisfaction 19.1%. — [기자협회보 61442](http://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61442)
- 64.7% are anxious that AI will reduce job security or status. By age: 50s+ 73.4%, 40s 66.0%, 30s 60.0%, 20s 58.7%. — [기자협회보 61441](http://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61441)
- 87.4% say AI improves reporting and production efficiency. — [기자협회보 61441](http://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61441)
- AI uses and newsroom AI measures are listed under Q2 below.

### Inferences
- Korean reporters face high output quotas: about 23.6 pieces a week, or 4–5 per working day. Wire and internet reporters face 27–38. Combined with falling morale, this means any tool that saves minutes per article (drafting, PR-release triage, fact lookup) touches the main daily burden.
- "Workload" is the 4th-ranked morale factor, after pay and status factors. A newsroom tool can address workload and achievement ("time for in-depth reporting") but not pay.
- For a trade outlet like 청년의사, the "internet outlet" profile is probably the closest comparator: high weekly counts, high WFH share and high AI use. This is inference only.

### Gaps
- The 2025 '한국의 언론인' daily or weekly working-hours figure and any stress index were not found in accessible coverage. The KPF report PDF itself was not retrieved.
- No Korean survey found itemises time spent per task (e.g., reading press releases, monitoring, transcription).
- No Korean data specific to trade or medical-specialty press (전문지) staff was found.

## 2. How are Korean reporters actually using generative AI in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
Adoption is mainstream: 58–62% use AI at work in the KPF and academic surveys, and only 8% say they never use it in the KJA July 2026 poll. Use clusters around data research and analysis, transcription, translation, headlines and copy-editing rather than writing article body text. Only about 36% of newsrooms have AI guidelines. Reporters' top demands are subscription and tool access, training, better tools and guidelines. Accuracy (error risk), trust and copyright are the main worries. A real failure case is Chosun Ilbo's AI copy-editing rollout, which increased corrections and workload.

### Cited Findings
**KJA July 2026 poll (n=1,625)** — [기자협회보 61441](http://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61441)
- AI uses (multiple response):

| Use | Share |
|---|---|
| Data research and analysis | 58.6% |
| Headline suggestions | 39.9% |
| Transcription and summarizing | 36.6% |
| Translation | 34.3% |
| Drafting and summarizing articles | 30.9% |
| Copyediting | 28.0% |
| Audio, video and image generation | 24.3% |
| Fact-checking | 20.3% |
| Story-idea generation | 13.8% |
| Breaking-news and foreign-press response | 8.1% |
| Layout and distribution | 3.2% |
| Do not use AI | 8.0% |

- Employer measures (multiple response):

| Measure | Share |
|---|---|
| Reporting-support AI tools launched | 39.6% |
| AI guidelines or code | 35.7% |
| Nothing applicable | 23.5% |
| Dedicated AI team | 22.7% |
| Broadcast/publishing AI tools | 13.6% |
| Labour agreement on AI | 6.7% |

- Demands on employers:

| Demand | Share |
|---|---|
| Subscription support and training | 64.6% |
| Upgrade and diversify AI tools | 46.0% |
| Guidelines and standards | 41.1% |
| Consult staff before adoption | 27.7% |
| Job guarantees | 23.3% |
| Address AI-company copyright infringement | 22.9% |
| Zero-click platform strategy | 14.0% |

- 74.7% expect a positive impact. Reasons: efficiency 78.9% and time for in-depth reporting 65.7%. Among the 360 with a negative view, the reasons are lower audience trust 65.3%, higher error risk 61.7% and job insecurity 61.1%.

**Academic survey (Park Aran, Shin Hyerin, Lee Nayeon; n=418 Korean journalists; online, 16 Aug–14 Sep, apparently 2024; reported 2025)** — [기자협회보 57446](http://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=57446)
- 62.2% have used generative AI at work. By desk: science/IT 80.0%, digital news team 79.4%, local/national 51.0%, society 44.6%.
- Among users, tasks used: speech-to-text transcription 84.0%, translating foreign materials 74.9%, typo/grammar checks 68.9%, searching for reporting material and cases 59.6%.
- Only 22.0% agree with using AI-generated text as part of an article, and 48.1% disagree. Reactions to AI fact-checking and idea generation were net negative (no % given).
- High concern about copyright infringement, false information and personal-rights violations (no %). Respondents expected weaker fact-checking, declining writing skills and a widening gap between outlets that can build AI tools and those that cannot.

**KPF small-sample study**
- About 70% of 103 Korean journalists use generative AI in work. Internet outlets, national dailies and economic papers/wires are higher, and regional dailies around 50%. The study is from the KPF open-source LLM report, registered 29 Dec 2025. — reported via search summary of [KPF research page](https://www.kpf.or.kr/front/research/selfDetail.do?seq=598951). The exact report URL and figures were not verified in the primary PDF, so treat with caution.

**Newsroom implementations (기자협회보, 2026)** — [기자협회보 61238](https://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61238)
- Seoul Economic Daily's in-house "AI NOVA" supports ideation, drafting, revision and copy-editing, headlines and graphics. About 300 reporters use it.
- Hankyung and E-Today use AI to analyse price data and **public disclosure filings (공시)**.
- Yonhap turns data files into charts. Its AI assistant drafts personnel, obituary and appointment items in house style. It planned a Q&A service over decades of archived articles and reporter notes, and is building multilingual STT for press conferences.
- Segye Ilbo reporters built a URL-to-card-news and short-video tool.

**Failure and backlash case**
- Chosun Ilbo disbanded its copy-editing team and fully adopted AI copy-editing in December 2025. Corrections ("바로잡습니다") rose to 20 in November and 29 in December. In a union survey (n not given), 79.6% felt typos had increased and 70.3% said their workload had grown. The union says AI misses context, and number-heavy economic news is a concern. The company disputes this. — [반론보도닷컴 주간 미디어동향, 29 Jan 2026](https://www.banronbodo.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=31659)
- The Gyeonggi Journalists Club adopted a generative AI guideline: AI as support only, mandatory reporter verification, disclosure, and an AI ethics committee. — [반론보도닷컴](https://www.banronbodo.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=31659)
- The three terrestrial broadcasters are suing Naver over AI training on news (about 97,000 works listed). — [반론보도닷컴](https://www.banronbodo.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=31659)
- At regional broadcaster G1, a duty reporter focused on AI news production during the April wildfire and treated the fire only as a short item, which was criticised internally. Labour and management then wrote an "AI news response manual." — [기자협회보 61238](https://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61238)

**Audience side (relevant to disclosure)**
- In a KPF Media Survey (Sept 2025; 1,000 adults aged 20–60s aware of generative AI), about 6 in 10 said articles using AI-generated images are "not news." — [기자협회보 59349](https://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=59349)

### Inferences
- Korean reporters' heaviest AI use is in exactly the "assistant" tasks 청년의사's app already covers or could cover: data research and analysis, transcription, summarisation and translation. Drafting article body text is less accepted (22% agree). So AI drafts should be framed as editable scaffolds, with provenance and citations visible.
- Error risk (61.7%) and the Chosun case suggest a feature that flags numbers, names and quotes in AI output for verification against source documents would directly answer the top concern.
- "Subscription support and training" (64.6%) suggests many reporters lack paid tools. An internal app with built-in LLM access fills that gap.

### Gaps
- No Korean survey found gives the frequency (daily or weekly) of AI use or time saved.
- No 2025–26 Korean figure was found for the share of journalists who have experienced AI hallucination errors.

## 3. What do global surveys report as top pain points and desired tools?

### Takeaway
Globally, journalists are flooded with irrelevant PR pitches: 55% receive more than 5 per day, 72% say fewer than a quarter are relevant, and 86% ignore off-beat pitches. Yet they rely on PR for story ideas (66%). They report expanding duties (62%) and exhaustion (47%), and name shrinking resources and workload (49%) and misinformation and verification as the top challenges. AI use is 53–77% depending on the survey, dominated by ChatGPT, transcription and writing aids. Media leaders' AI priority is "back-end automation" (97% call it important), while 42% say results so far are limited.

### Cited Findings
**Muck Rack State of Journalism 2025 (n=1,515 after cleaning of 1,890; April 2025; mostly US)** — [Muck Rack PDF](https://media.muckrack.com/documents/6.9.2025_state_of_journalism.pdf)
- Top concerns (pick 3): disinformation 36%, public trust 28%, lack of funding 28%, polarization 25%, government interference 23%, decline of local news 21%, **understaffing and time pressure 20%**, unregulated AI 18%, **information overload and audience fragmentation 17%**, **quality control and fact-checking capacity 10%**.
- Feelings about the work: meaningful 67%, **exhausting 47%**, precarious 33%, routine or repetitive 24%.
- **62% say their job responsibilities expanded beyond their core role** in the past year (27% significantly). 37% had layoffs or buyouts at their organization.
- Time to do work to their standards: always 19%, often 40%, sometimes 29%, rarely 11%, never 1%.
- AI tools used in the past 12 months: any 77%, ChatGPT 42%, transcription tools 40%, Grammarly and other writing tools 35%, Gemini 13%, **newsroom-built AI tools 11%**, Perplexity 8%, Claude 6%.
- PR pitches on the last normal workday: 1–5 46%, 6–10 25%, 11–20 17% (up from 11% in 2024), 21+ 13%. So **55% receive more than 5 per day**.
- Relevance: pitches are relevant always 3%, usually 16%, about half the time 33%, seldom 42%, never 5%. 54% seldom or never respond to pitches.
- Share of stories that start with a PR pitch: 0% for 16%, 1–10% for 49%, 11–25% for 20%, 26–50% for 11%, over 50% for 4%.
- What makes a pitch worth time: tailored to beat 69%, credible source access 44%, new or exclusive info 41%, brief 31%, timely 26%, data 23%.
- What pitches should include: relevance 66%, interview access 62%, original data 42%, images 36%, **exclusive or embargoed content 33%**.
- Reasons to delete a pitch instantly: irrelevant 86%, promotional 71%, mass-email look 50%, repeated without response 44%, too long 43%.

**Muck Rack State of Work-Life Balance in Journalism 2025 (n=432; Jan–Feb 2025; mostly US)**
- Half considered quitting in the past year due to exhaustion and burnout (56% in the 2024 edition). 42% have previously left a job due to burnout. 37% say workload is the biggest barrier to taking vacation. 38% say their mental health worsened. — [Muck Rack PDF](https://media.muckrack.com/documents/3.17.2025_SoWLB_-_journo_report.pdf); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/404539/many-journalists-suffer-high-stress-burnout-level.html) (figures via search summaries; PDF not parsed directly)

**Cision 2026 State of the Media (n=1,899; Jan–Feb 2026; 19 markets incl. APAC)** — [Cision press release](https://www.cision.com/about/press-releases/2026-press-releases/pr-emerges-as-the-primary-source-for-journalists-in-high-pressure-newsrooms-302770936/)
- **72% say fewer than a quarter of pitches they receive are relevant.**
- 66% rely on PR content (press releases, pitches, media kits) for story ideas.
- **49% cite shrinking budgets, staff cuts and increased workload as their biggest obstacle.**
- Accuracy, fact-checking and combating misinformation is the No. 1 challenge (no % in the release).
- 53% oppose AI-generated pitches. Embargoed information is among the most valued pitch elements (no % given).
- Caveat: vendor research.

**Cision 2025 State of the Media (n=3,000+; 19 markets)** — [PR Newswire blog](https://mediablog.prnewswire.com/2025/05/14/2025-state-of-the-media-takeaways/)
- Half receive more than 50 pitches per week.
- 86% reject pitches for lack of relevance. 78% would block a PR person for spamming irrelevant pitches.
- 53% use generative AI and 14% plan to. AI tasks: researching topics 25%, transcription 23%, summarizing 20%. AI for outlines and early drafts: APAC 41%, EMEA 16%, North America 11%.
- Top challenge: adapting to changing audience behaviour 42%.
- Secondary summaries report that 72% are concerned about factual inaccuracies in AI content and 60% feel the strain of downsizing. These could not be confirmed in the primary source. — [Cision APAC article](https://www.cision.asia/resources/articles/2025-state-of-the-media-report-apac-journalists-have-a-type-its-called-relevant/)

**Reuters Institute, "AI adoption by UK journalists and their newsrooms" (n=1,004 UK journalists; Aug–Nov 2024; published Nov 2025)** — [RISJ](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/ai-adoption-uk-journalists-and-their-newsrooms-surveying-applications-approaches-and-attitudes)
- 56% use AI professionally at least weekly, 27% less often, 16% never.
- At least monthly use: transcription and captioning 49%, translation 33%, grammar and copy-editing 30%. Story research is about 22% and idea or headline generation about 16% (from secondary summaries).
- 62% perceive AI as a threat to journalism. — [Oxford Politics news](https://politics.ox.ac.uk/news/most-uk-journalists-perceive-ai-threat-new-risj-research-reveals)

**Reuters Institute, Journalism, Media and Technology Trends and Predictions 2026 (280 digital leaders, 51 countries; Nov–Dec 2025)** — [RISJ](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/journalism-media-and-technology-trends-and-predictions-2026)
- Share of leaders rating each AI use important: back-end automation 97% (transcription, copy-editing assistance, metadata, style guides built into CMS), newsgathering 82%, coding and product 81%.
- AI initiatives so far: transformational 13%, promising 44%, limited 42%.
- 67% have not saved any jobs through AI.
- Only 38% are confident about journalism's future, down from 60% a year earlier.
- Examples: Reuters' FactGenie extracts facts from press releases and halved alert times. iTromsø's "Djinn" sifts government documents. Helsingin Sanomat bots monitor Telegram channels.

**WAN-IFRA (older, 2023)**
- Only 20% of newsrooms had generative AI guidelines. 49% let journalists use tools as they see fit. — [Press Gazette, 2023](https://pressgazette.co.uk/publishers/digital-journalism/wan-ifra-generative-ai-newsroom/)
- No 2025 WAN-IFRA guideline percentage was found.

**ICFJ State of Technology in Global Newsrooms (older; ~2017; 2,700+ respondents, 130 countries)**
- 52% of journalists wanted data journalism training, but only 40% of newsrooms offered it. Fewer than one-third of newsrooms use advanced digital skills such as data journalism. — [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/?p=148617) (year inferred, verify)

### Inferences
- The global and Korean pictures converge. There are too many incoming items (PR or press releases), low relevance, more duties per person, and AI used mainly for "language plumbing" (transcription, translation, summaries) rather than writing.
- Muck Rack's "newsroom-built AI tools 11%" suggests in-house tools such as 청년의사's app are still rare. They are differentiated where they integrate the newsroom's own data sources.
- Fact-checking and accuracy appear as the No. 1 challenge (Cision 2026) and the top AI worry (Korea, error risk 61.7%). Verification support is under-served.

### Gaps
- No global survey found quantifies hours spent per week on press-release triage or monitoring specifically.
- The Reuters Digital News Report 2025/2026 is an audience survey and was not mined here. It has no journalist workload data.
- The JournalismAI/LSE 2025 report was not retrieved.

## 4. Friction around press releases, monitoring, embargoes, verifying numbers and turning data into stories

### Takeaway
Korean data on press-release volume is dated but consistent. Regional reporters received about 18 releases a day and used about 35% (2017 study). Beat reporters face 10–20 a day (2020 KPF study). Release-driven, near-identical coverage is a known pathology. Embargo breaches are a live 2025 problem; they lead to beat-access suspensions and to ministries watermarking releases. Data on verification and data-analysis skill gaps in Korea is thin. Globally, data journalism training demand exceeds supply.

### Cited Findings
**Press-release volume and use**
- Survey of 238 reporters at 14 Gwangju outlets (Kwon Shin-oh doctoral study; 2017 article): an average of **18.43 releases received per day**, of which **6.53 (35.44%) were used** in articles. Of releases used, 44.8% were rewritten (indirect use), 18.9% quoted verbatim and 36.2% used as reference. Usage rates were newspapers 40.37%, wire 30.57% and broadcast 25.57%. — [기자협회보 41098 (2017)](https://www.journalist.or.kr/m/m_article.html?no=41098)
- 2020 KPF study (Park Jae-young et al.): an IT/electronics beat reporter receives more than 10 releases a day plus 2–3 briefings a week. A National Assembly reporter writes 2–3 articles a day. At the Guardian, about 1 in 10 releases becomes a story. — [미디어스 (Nov 2020)](https://www.mediaus.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=198505)
- A search summary of the same study cited "10–20 releases per beat per day" and "3–4 articles per day per beat reporter." These exact figures were not found in the fetched articles, so treat as unverified.
- An industry interview: an industrial-beat reporter says releases number "dozens" a day, so he reads only titles and prioritises his beat's own releases. An IT reporter says a well-prepared release takes 30 minutes to write up, but one needing verification takes 3 hours. — [maily.so PR letter](https://maily.so/prletter/posts/knrj2364zld) (PR-industry newsletter, anecdotal; exact page attribution unverified)
- Claims of "50+ releases per day" or "hundreds of emails" appear in PR-industry blogs without a clear source, so they are unverified. — [i-boss](https://www.i-boss.co.kr/ab-6141-25312)

**Embargoes**
- October 2025: the government's "10·15 real-estate measures" release leaked early. Because MOLIT watermarks each release with outlet and reporter, the leak was traced, and the press corps voted a 6-month beat suspension for TV Chosun. In August 2024 a MOLIT housing-supply release leak led to a 1-year suspension for JoongAng Ilbo. The Financial Services Commission does not watermark, which limits investigation. Reporters worry they may stop receiving embargoed material. — [기자협회보 59567](https://www.journalist.or.kr/ezview/article_main.html?no=59567)
- KJA ethics column proposes calling embargo "보도유예," which reflects ongoing confusion around the term. — [기자협회보 52442](https://www.journalist.or.kr/m/m_article.html?no=52442)
- Globally, 33% of journalists want exclusive or embargoed content with pitches (Muck Rack 2025). Cision 2026 lists embargoed info among the most valued pitch elements. — [Muck Rack PDF](https://media.muckrack.com/documents/6.9.2025_state_of_journalism.pdf); [Cision 2026](https://www.cision.com/about/press-releases/2026-press-releases/pr-emerges-as-the-primary-source-for-journalists-in-high-pressure-newsrooms-302770936/)

**Verification and numbers**
- 61.7% of Korean journalists who are negative about AI cite higher error risk. 20.3% already use AI for fact-checking (KJA 2026). — [기자협회보 61441](http://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61441)
- The AI copy-editing rollout raised errors; the union flagged number-heavy economic stories as a risk (Chosun, 2025–26). — [반론보도닷컴](https://www.banronbodo.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=31659)
- Globally, "quality control and fact-checking capacity" is a top-3 concern for 10% (Muck Rack 2025). Accuracy and fact-checking is the No. 1 challenge (Cision 2026). — sources as above
- An EBU study cited by RISJ found that ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini misrepresent news content almost half the time. — [RISJ Trends 2026](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/journalism-media-and-technology-trends-and-predictions-2026)

**Data analysis and data-to-story**
- "Data research and analysis" is the No. 1 AI use among Korean reporters (58.6%), which implies demand for analysis help. — [기자협회보 61441](http://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61441)
- Korean outlets (Hankyung, E-Today) use AI to analyse public disclosures and price data. Yonhap auto-generates charts from data files. — [기자협회보 61238](https://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=61238)
- Globally, 42% of journalists want original data or research included with pitches and 23% say data makes a pitch worth their time (Muck Rack 2025). In an older ICFJ survey (~2017), data-training demand was 52% against 40% supply. — sources as above

**Monitoring and information overload**
- 17% of journalists globally rank "information overload and audience fragmentation" among their top 3 concerns (Muck Rack 2025). — [Muck Rack PDF](https://media.muckrack.com/documents/6.9.2025_state_of_journalism.pdf)
- RISJ 2026 examples of AI-driven source monitoring: Helsingin Sanomat's Telegram bots and iTromsø's Djinn sifting municipal documents. — [RISJ Trends 2026](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/journalism-media-and-technology-trends-and-predictions-2026)

### Inferences
- About 18 releases a day with about 35% use means roughly 12 discarded releases per reporter per day (2017 Gwangju data). Relevance scoring and dedup against already-claimed or published stories is the obvious lever. 청년의사's digest plus triage inbox already addresses this; an "already covered by competitor or us" check would extend it.
- Embargo handling in Korea carries severe sanctions (6–12-month beat suspensions). Automated embargo-time detection with a hard publish-lock or countdown in the triage inbox has real risk-reduction value.
- Verification support is the biggest unmet need: checking AI-draft numbers against the source PDF or disclosure, and diffing a release against prior statements. This is inference.

### Gaps
- No 2024–26 Korean survey on press-release volume per reporter was found. The best Korean figures are from 2017 and 2020.
- No Korean data on time spent verifying figures or on data-skills gaps among reporters was found.
- No health/medical-specific data on press releases from pharma, hospitals or the ministry (보건복지부, 식약처) was found.

## 5. Pain points specific to Korean beat reporters (출입처, 기자단, briefings, release dependence, 받아쓰기)

### Takeaway
The 2020 KPF study on the beat system found that 67–82% of reporters at major outlets are tied to beats, and 70–93% of junior reporters (≤5 years). The result is release-driven "news floods" of near-identical stories alongside "news deserts." Reporters blame lack of time and staff, plus portal page-view competition. Foreign correspondents view release copying as not journalism.

### Cited Findings
- 2020 KPF report "언론사 출입처 제도와 취재 관행 연구" (Park Jae-young, Heo Man-seop, Ahn Su-chan). Share of reporters on beats: general daily 67.2% (of 157), economic daily 81.7% (of 141), broadcaster 70.4% (of 161). Among reporters with ≤5 years' experience: 91.3%, 70% and 92.8%. Over 120 reporters are registered at MOLIT alone. — [기자협회보 48480 (Nov 2020)](https://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=48480); [미디어스](https://www.mediaus.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=198505)
- Foreign comparison: the Guardian assigns 30–50 of 250–300 reporters to beats (about 5–10%). The NYT has beats only at the White House, Pentagon and Supreme Court. — [기자협회보 48480](https://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=48480)
- Asahi reporter Kamiya Takeshi: "어떤 기자는 보도자료를 '복붙'해서 기사를 쓴다. 그게 기자인가." Korean reporters attribute release dependence to limited time and staff. Portal page-view competition pushes announcement-based stories. — [기자협회보 48480](https://www.journalist.or.kr/news/article.html?no=48480)
- Researchers proposed limiting beats to about 10% of staff and creating dedicated release-processing roles. — [기자협회보 48488](http://journalist.or.kr/m/m_article.html?no=48488)
- Reporters from small or non-member outlets are frustrated at exclusion from closed press corps (기자단). — [기자협회보 39245](http://m.journalist.or.kr/m/m_article.html?no=39245) (older, year not verified)
- Embargo enforcement via press-corps votes and beat suspensions (2024–25 cases). — [기자협회보 59567](https://www.journalist.or.kr/ezview/article_main.html?no=59567)

### Inferences
- The proposed "dedicated release-processing" function is exactly what AI digesting and drafting can play. For a 30–40 person trade paper with presumably thin beat coverage, automating release-to-brief conversion frees reporters for enterprise stories. That counters the 받아쓰기 criticism instead of amplifying it, provided drafts surface what is new versus the release and prompt for additional sourcing.
- A trade paper outside major 기자단 may receive government embargoed material inconsistently. Monitoring official sites (ministries, 심평원, 건보공단 notices) can partly compensate.

### Gaps
- No 2024–26 quantitative update on 출입처 staffing or release dependence was found.
- No data was found on how health and welfare beat press corps (보건복지부 기자단) treat trade-press reporters.
